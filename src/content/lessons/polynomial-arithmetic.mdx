The last lesson covered what a polynomial is. **This one covers what you can do with it** — adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing polynomials, which turns out to be exactly the same journey plain numbers took a few lessons back, just one level up.

## Adding polynomials

Adding two polynomials means grouping their matching powers of $x$ together and adding the coefficients that sit on each one.

<Figure caption="Fig. 1 — grouping matching powers of x, then adding the coefficient in each group.">
  <PolynomialAddFigure />
</Figure>

<MathBox label="Addition">

$$(3x^2 + 2x + 5) + (x^2 + 4x + 1) = 4x^2 + 6x + 6$$

</MathBox>

## Subtracting polynomials

Subtraction works the same way, with one extra move first: **flip the sign of every term in the second polynomial**, then add as usual.

<Figure caption="Fig. 2 — flip every sign in the second polynomial first, then group and combine exactly like addition.">
  <PolynomialSubtractFigure />
</Figure>

<MathBox label="Subtraction">

$$(3x^2 + 2x + 5) - (x^2 + 4x + 1) = 2x^2 - 2x + 4$$

</MathBox>

## Multiplying polynomials

Multiplying two polynomials means every term in the first one multiplies every term in the second — then the products get combined.

<Figure caption="Fig. 3 — every term in (x + 2) multiplied against every term in (x + 3), then summed.">
  <PolynomialMultiplyFigure />
</Figure>

<MathBox label="Multiplication">

$$(x + 2)(x + 3) = x^2 + 3x + 2x + 6 = x^2 + 5x + 6$$

</MathBox>

## Doing this over a finite field

This is where the finite-fields lesson connects back in. In a ZK system, every coefficient lives inside $GF(p)$ — so after multiplying, every coefficient gets reduced mod $p$, same as it would for a plain number.

<Figure caption="Fig. 4 — the same multiplication process, finished off with one extra step: reduce every coefficient mod 7.">
  <PolynomialMultiplyFieldFigure />
</Figure>

<MathBox label="Multiplication over GF(7)">

$$(x + 5)(x + 4) \equiv x^2 + 2x + 6 \pmod{7}$$

$9 \bmod 7 = 2$ and $20 \bmod 7 = 6$ — every coefficient wraps around exactly like it did two lessons back, just attached to a power of $x$ instead of standing alone.

</MathBox>

## Dividing polynomials

Division is the trickiest of the four, but it runs on the same long-division process as plain numbers: divide the leading terms, multiply back, subtract, and repeat.

<Figure caption="Fig. 5 — long division of x² - 5x + 6 by x - 2, the same polynomial whose roots got checked by hand last lesson.">
  <PolynomialDivideFigure />
</Figure>

<MathBox label="Division">

$$(x^2 - 5x + 6) \div (x - 2) = x - 3, \quad \text{remainder } 0$$

A remainder of $0$ isn't a coincidence here — $x - 2$ divides in cleanly because $2$ is one of this polynomial's roots, found the same way back in the previous lesson.

</MathBox>

## Why division matters for ZK

That connection between roots and clean division is the entire reason this operation matters so much for ZK systems.

<Figure caption="Fig. 6 — dividing by (x - root) leaves no remainder exactly when that value really is a root.">
  <PolynomialZKDivisionFigure />
</Figure>

$p(x) \div (x - \text{root})$ leaves a remainder of $0$ **if and only if** that value really is a root of $p(x)$ — a zero remainder means a valid computation, a nonzero one means it isn't. **This exact check is how a QAP verifies a ZK witness**: not by recomputing everything, just by confirming one polynomial division comes out clean.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Adding or subtracting polynomials means combining the coefficients on matching powers of $x$.
- Multiplying polynomials means every term in one multiplies every term in the other, then the products get combined.
- Division runs the same long-division process plain numbers do: divide the leading terms, multiply back, subtract, repeat.
- Over a finite field, every coefficient gets reduced mod $p$ after the arithmetic, exactly like any other number in $GF(p)$.
- $p(x) \div (x - \text{root})$ leaves remainder $0$ if and only if that value really is a root — the exact check a QAP uses to verify a ZK witness.

</KeyTakeaways>
